## 2. Two findings that change the plan

**Finding 1 — the "get into roundups" tactic has almost no inventory here.**

dominant "best jewelry redesign services" listicle. Page 1 is entirely individual jewelers'

**So invert it: become the resource instead of chasing inclusion in one.** Nobody owns the

**Finding 2 — podcasts are the proven play in this exact niche.**
Melanie Casey's strongest real links come from a podcast appearance: `iheart.com` (38 links),

For a solo maker with a genuinely good story — heirlooms, grief, divorce, sustainability,
woman-owned SF studio — podcasts are the highest-yield, lowest-competition channel available.
Hosts need guests every week. There is no gatekeeper.

## 3. Strategy

**The principle: relevance beats authority at your size.** One link from a jewelry blog that
nobody's heard of is worth more to you than a mention on a huge irrelevant site. Five to ten
real links change your rankings. Fifty junk ones change nothing.

**Three horizons, run in parallel but weighted differently:**

| Horizon | Speed | Ceiling | Weight now |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Local** (SF/Bay Area) | Fast — weeks | Low but compounding; feeds the Map Pack | 30% |
| **Vertical** (jewelry industry + niche media) | Medium — 1–3 months | Medium, highly relevant | 50% |
| **National PR** | Slow — 3–12 months | Highest | 20% |

**Targets:** 5 real referring domains by 1 Nov 2026 · 15 by Feb 2027 · 40 by Aug 2027.
Count only links a human editor chose to place. Junk doesn't count.

**What not to do:** don't buy links, don't use "guest post packages," don't submit to generic
web directories, don't chase volume. Your profile already carries a spam score of 36 — adding
more junk actively hurts.

## 4. Tactical map, in priority order

### ① Podcast guesting — start here
**Why:** competitor-proven, no gatekeeper, one appearance yields many links, and Stacey's story
is the product. Perfect fit for someone who is better talking than writing.

**Targets:** jewelry/maker podcasts (search "jewelry podcast", "maker podcast", "craft business
podcast"), grief and divorce-recovery podcasts (your ICP 2/3 registers — genuinely underserved),
sustainability/slow-fashion shows, SF/Bay Area small-business and woman-founder shows.

**Pitch angles that land:** "what people actually do with a dead spouse's wedding ring" ·
"the ring didn't change, I did" · "why redesign beats buying new" · "twenty years at the bench."

**Asset needed:** a one-page speaker sheet — bio, three story angles, headshot, before/after
images, link. *(Does not exist. Claude can draft it.)*
**Effort:** 30 min per pitch · **Yield:** 1 real link + 5–20 directory links per booking

### ② Local citations and the GBP ecosystem
**Why:** cheapest, fastest, and it compounds with the Map Pack push already underway.
"Custom jeweler near me" is 8,100/mo and you're not in it.

**Targets, in order:** Google Business Profile (in verification) → Bing Places → Apple Business
Connect → Yelp → Nextdoor business page → SF Chamber of Commerce → Bay Area maker/artisan
directories → local "woman-owned business" lists.

**Asset needed:** a NAP sheet — one exact Name, Address, Phone string used identically
everywhere. Inconsistency actively hurts local ranking. *(Does not exist. Claude can build it.)*
**Effort:** ~20 min each · **Yield:** low authority each, but they stack and feed local rankings

### ③ Referral partners — links *and* leads
**Why:** the only channel that produces revenue and authority from the same conversation. The
one-pagers are already drafted in `content-drafts/referral-onepager-*.md`.

**Targets:** divorce attorneys (ICP 2) · estate and probate attorneys (ICP 4) · financial
advisors and appraisers · wedding planners · antique dealers and estate-sale companies.

**The ask:** a "trusted partners" or "resources" page link, reciprocated.
**Effort:** highest per link — real relationships · **Yield:** 1–2 links/month, plus actual clients

### ④ Directories — the complete verified list

**Prerequisite: build the NAP sheet first.** One exact Name / Address / Phone string, used
identically everywhere. Inconsistent NAP across directories actively *hurts* local ranking, and
it is painful to correct after the fact.

**Privacy note first — decide before submitting anywhere.** If the studio is a home address,
register everywhere as a **service-area business** and hide the street address. Publishing a
home address across a dozen directories is effectively irreversible.

#### Tier 1 — the foundation (do all five)
These drive the majority of local search signal and are increasingly what AI assistants cite
when answering local queries.

| Directory | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Google Business Profile** | In verification. Everything else is secondary to this. |
| **Apple Business Connect** | Powers Apple Maps/Siri. Free, frequently skipped. |
| **Bing Places** | Free, and feeds Copilot answers. |
| **Yelp** | Claim and complete even if you never court reviews there. |
| **Nextdoor** | Business page free; hyperlocal, matches the 35–65 homeowner ICP. |

#### Tier 2 — the ones that actually fit Lorinczi
Higher relevance than Tier 1 for link purposes, because these are *jewelry* and *maker* contexts.

| Directory | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| **SFMade makers' directory** (`sfmade.org/makers-directory`) | SF manufacturer/maker directory that explicitly lists jewelry designers working in small batches with low-waste processes. Local + maker + sustainability in one listing — the single best-fit directory on this page. |
| **Ethical Metalsmiths** | 501(c)3 with "Responsible Professional Jeweler" member profiles. Direct match for the reuse-your-own-gold and refining story. |
| **Metal Arts Guild of San Francisco** (`metalartsguildsf.org`) | SF metalsmith guild — local peer community, likely referral source as well as a link. |
| **Ganoksin business directory** (`ganoksin.com/business-directory/`) | The jewelry-making community hub. |
| **MJSA** (`mjsa.org`) | Manufacturing Jewelers & Suppliers of America, trade body since 1903. |
| **American Jewelry Design Council** | Non-profit promoting original jewelry design as art. |

#### Tier 3 — only if time allows
Better Business Bureau · Foursquare · SF Chamber of Commerce · local "woman-owned business" lists.

#### Deliberately skipped — and why

- **The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola.** The obvious "jewelry" directories, and the wrong audience.
  They are bride-facing — women in their twenties buying a *first* ring. Your ICP already owns
  the jewelry. Same demographic mismatch as the Pinterest audience problem. Skip unless the
  business deliberately pivots toward bridal.
- **Thumbtack and pay-per-lead marketplaces.** Price-shopping buyers, wrong for a bespoke
  consultative sale. (Reasoned through separately — see the Nextdoor/Thumbtack assessment.)
- **"150+ citation site" bulk submissions.** ⚠️ **This is how the current spam problem
  happened.** `simplewebdirectory.com`, `smartwebdirectory.com`, `australianwebdirectory.pro`
  and `globalecommerce.org` are *already in your backlink profile* and are part of what's
  dragging the spam score to 36. Never buy a bulk citation package. Eleven good listings beat
  one hundred and fifty junk ones, and the junk actively costs you.

**Effort:** 20–60 min each; some Tier 2 entries carry paid membership · **Yield:** permanent,
relevant, and they compound with the Map Pack push

### ⑤ Peer and vendor cross-links
**Why:** Melanie Casey has links from a wedding photographer and three other jewelers. Jewelry
is a referral trade; people link to people they work with.

**Targets:** photographers who shoot your pieces · SF metalsmiths and lapidaries you already
use · casting houses and stone dealers · other jewelers who *don't* do redesign and could refer
it out (most don't — it's fiddly, low-margin work for them and core business for you).

**Effort:** low — these are existing relationships · **Yield:** underrated, start immediately

### ⑥ HARO / Qwoted / Connectively
**Why:** the standard ongoing tactic. Journalists post requests daily; jewelry, weddings,
sustainability and personal-finance queries all fit.
**Effort:** 15 min/day scanning, 10 min per response · **Yield:** slow, occasionally a big one

### ⑦ Guest content on supplier and niche blogs
**Why:** `esslinger.com` — a jewelry tools supplier — gives Melanie Casey 14 article links.
Supplier blogs need content and have no competitive conflict with you.
**Targets:** jewelry supply companies, gemstone dealers, sustainability blogs, SF maker publications.
**Effort:** 2–4 hrs per piece · **Yield:** 1 strong contextual link each

### ⑧ Community — Reddit and Quora
**Why:** doesn't build much authority directly, but ranks and gets cited by AI assistants.
The disclosed-jeweler answer pack already exists in `content-drafts/reddit-answers.md`.
**Effort:** 20 min/week · **Yield:** referral traffic and AI citations more than link equity

### ⑨ Unlinked mention reclamation — **currently blocked**
`TRAFFIC-EXECUTION-PLAN.md` cites Elle, Brides, Marie Claire and W. **None appear as backlinks,
and no URLs are recorded anywhere in the repo.** A brand-mention search was a dead end —
"Lorinczi" is a common Hungarian surname, so results are dominated by unrelated authors and
musicians.
**Unblock:** Stacey supplies the actual article URLs. If those features are real and unlinked,
this is the single cheapest authority win available. Set a Google Alert for "Lorinczi jewelry"
to catch future mentions.

## 5. The content you need

Links are earned by assets. Here's what exists, what's missing, and what to build.

### Already working — promote, don't rebuild
- **The cost guide** (`/pages/jewelry-redesign-cost`) — already holds 12 rankings on the
  reset-cost cluster. Proven demand. This is your link magnet; every pitch should point at it.
- **Before/after gallery** — the most linkable thing a jeweler owns. Visual, concrete, shareable.

### Missing, in build order

**1. Speaker sheet** *(unblocks channel ①, highest priority)*
One page: bio, three story angles, headshot, two before/afters, contact. Half a day.

**2. NAP sheet** *(unblocks channel ②)*
One canonical Name/Address/Phone string. Trivial to make, expensive to get wrong.

**3. Press kit page on the site** *(supports ①, ⑥, ⑨)*
`/pages/press` — high-res images, bio, fact sheet, past coverage. Journalists check for this
before writing; not having one costs placements silently.

**4. The original-data asset — the highest ceiling in this document**
Journalists and bloggers link to **data nobody else has**. You have twenty years of client
work and nobody in this niche has published anything like it.

*"The Redesign Report" — what 200 women actually did with jewelry they stopped wearing.*
What percentage were inherited vs. divorce vs. outgrown · what they chose (melt vs. rework) ·
how long they waited before deciding · what it cost versus buying new.

Anonymised, aggregated, no client identified. This is the one asset that could earn national
press links rather than chasing them — and it's defensible, because only Stacey has the data.
**Effort:** genuinely significant. **Ceiling:** far higher than everything else here combined.

**5. "What to do with inherited jewelry" — the resource that doesn't exist**
Since no neutral roundup owns this niche (§2), write the definitive one: all the options,
including the ones you don't profit from (sell it, keep it as-is, pass it on). Honesty about
non-purchase options is exactly what makes a page link-worthy and AI-citable.
