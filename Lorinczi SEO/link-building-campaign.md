# Link Building & Digital PR Campaign — Lorinczi Jewelry

**Goal:** Build backlink authority — the #1 ceiling on rankings (confirmed by the competitor teardown: Melanie Casey wins on authority, not page strategy).
**Baseline:** near-zero referring domains. **6-mo target:** 15 quality referring domains. **12-mo:** 40.
**Owner:** Stacey (relationships/outreach) · Claude (targets, templates, content, tracking).

> **Quality > quantity.** 5 relevant, real links from jewelry/local/design sites beat 100 directory spam links. Never buy links or use link farms — for a low-authority site that risks a penalty.

---

## The 7 link channels (in priority order)

### 1. 🎯 Niche roundups & "best of" lists — HIGHEST PRIORITY
These rank for your exact terms AND feed AI citations (ChatGPT/Perplexity pull from them). Getting listed = link + referral traffic + AI visibility.
**Targets to pitch (search "best jewelry redesign services", "where to reset a diamond", "heirloom redesign"):**
- Jewelry/wedding blogs that publish vendor roundups
- "Sustainable / recycled gold jewelry" lists (your eco angle fits)
- "Woman-owned jewelry brands" lists
- "Custom jewelers [San Francisco / Bay Area]" local roundups
**Action:** find the articles already ranking page 1 for these terms → email the author to be added (template B).

### 2. 📍 Local citations & directories (feeds #7 GBP + local SEO)
Consistent Name/Address/Phone (NAP) across these builds local authority:
- Google Business Profile (task #7 — the anchor)
- Bing Places, Apple Business Connect
- Yelp, Nextdoor (SF Bay Area)
- Local SF/Bay Area business directories & chambers
- **Jewelry-specific:** designer guild directories, MJSA, Ganoksin artisan listings
**Action:** create/claim each with identical NAP. Low effort, compounding local signal.

### 3. 📰 Digital PR / press features
Story angles journalists want (you're a woman-owned SF studio reimagining heirlooms — strong human-interest hooks):
- "Turning grandmother's ring into a modern heirloom" (sentimental)
- "Sustainable jewelry: why redesign beats buying new" (eco)
- "Meet the maker" local-designer profile
**Outlets:** SF lifestyle press, local magazines, jewelry trade press, design/craft blogs, sustainability publications.
**Tools:** sign up for **Connectively (formerly HARO)**, **Qwoted**, **SourceBottle** — journalists post requests daily; respond to jewelry/wedding/sustainability queries (template C). This is the single best ongoing tactic for a small brand.

### 4. 🤝 Referral partnerships (links + actual leads)
Partner with businesses whose clients need redesign:
- **Estate attorneys / probate** (inherited jewelry) ← highest fit
- **Wedding planners** (engagement ring redesign, heirloom for "something old")
- **Antique dealers & estate sale companies**
- **Financial advisors / appraisers**
**Action:** offer a reciprocal "trusted partner" page link + referral arrangement (template D).

### 5. ✍️ Guest content
Write for jewelry/wedding/lifestyle blogs with a byline link:
- "How to redesign an heirloom without losing its meaning"
- "What to do with inherited jewelry you'll never wear"
(Your existing blog posts prove you can produce this — repurpose angles for guest spots.)

### 6. 💬 Genuine community participation (no spam)
- **Reddit:** r/jewelry, r/EngagementRings, r/AskWomenOver30 — answer redesign questions helpfully, link only when truly useful
- **Quora:** "What can I do with my grandmother's ring?" etc. — these rank and get cited by AI
- Facebook groups (jewelry, weddings, local Bay Area)

### 7. 🔗 Reclaim unlinked mentions
If anyone mentions "Lorinczi" without linking (press, blogs, social reposts), ask for a link. (Set a Google Alert for "Lorinczi jewelry" to catch these.)

## Outreach templates

**A — Roundup inclusion**
> Subject: Addition for your "[article title]" piece?
> Hi [name], I loved your roundup of [topic] — really useful for anyone with jewelry they no longer wear. I run Lorinczi Jewelry, a [SF] studio specializing in **jewelry redesign and heirloom resetting** (taking clients' own gems/gold and reimagining them). If you ever update the piece, I'd be honored to be considered — happy to share before/after photos and details. Either way, thanks for the great resource. — Stacey

**B — Press / story pitch**
> Subject: Story idea: turning inherited jewelry into pieces people actually wear
> Hi [name], I'm a [SF] jewelry designer and I've noticed a trend worth a story: people are redesigning inherited and unworn jewelry instead of buying new — for sentiment, sustainability, and cost. I've helped [X] clients turn a grandmother's ring or old gold into a piece they wear daily. Happy to share before/after photos and client stories if it's a fit. — Stacey, Lorinczi Jewelry

**C — HARO/Qwoted response** (keep tight, quotable)
> [Answer the exact question in 2-3 sentences, lead with the insight.] — Stacey Lorinczi, jewelry designer & founder of Lorinczi Jewelry ([SF]), specializing in jewelry redesign and heirloom resetting. [site]

**D — Referral partnership**
> Subject: A trusted redesign resource for your clients
> Hi [name], I work with many clients who've inherited jewelry they're unsure what to do with — and I imagine some of yours face the same. I'm a [SF] designer specializing in redesigning and resetting heirloom pieces. I'd love to be a resource you can refer to, and I'm glad to do the same for you. Open to a quick call? — Stacey
