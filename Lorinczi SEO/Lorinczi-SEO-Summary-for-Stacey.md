# Part 1 — The Strategy: "Capturing Organic Traffic"

## What "organic traffic" means

There are two ways people land on your website:

- **Paid traffic** — you pay for an ad, someone clicks, you stop paying, the visitors stop.
- **Organic traffic** — someone types a question into Google (or asks ChatGPT), and your site shows up *for free* because Google decided your page is the best answer.

Organic traffic is the goal. It compounds. A page that ranks keeps bringing in customers month after month without you paying per click. **The whole point of this project is to build a steady stream of free, high-intent visitors** — people who are actively looking for exactly what you do.

## Why we picked "jewelry redesign" instead of "custom jewelry"

We pulled real Google search data. Here's what it showed:

- On generic terms like *"custom jewelry,"* the search results are owned by national giants with enormous budgets. Your site, today, gets roughly **8 visitors a month** from Google and ranks for only **9 search terms** — none of them on page one. We cannot out-muscle Tiffany head-on.
- But there's a quieter, **emotionally charged niche with about 3,500–5,000 searches a month** that the giants largely ignore: people searching *"reset my diamond,"* *"redesign engagement ring,"* *"what to do with my grandmother's ring,"* *"repurpose old jewelry."*
- The sites currently winning those searches are **independent designers** — not national chains. That means they're **beatable**.
- And it's a *perfect* fit for your business: the people searching are overwhelmingly **women 45–70 who own inherited jewelry**, have the means to commission a redesign, and care about story and craftsmanship over a brand name on a box. They often supply their own gold and stones, which makes the work **higher-margin** — and the whole thing can be done **by mail**, so you never have to publish a studio address.

So the strategy in one sentence:

> **Own the jewelry-redesign niche — emotionally and on Google — and become *the* place people go when they have a piece they love but never wear.**

## The "AI search" angle (this is newer, and a real edge)

People increasingly ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google's AI things like *"what should I do with my late mother's ring?"* The redesign niche is exactly the kind of question-and-answer territory AI loves to summarize. We've structured your new content to be **clearly quotable and machine-readable**, so your site has a real shot at being *cited by AI* — something the big chains aren't optimizing for yet.

## B) Technical fixes under the hood (invisible to visitors, important to Google)

These are the kinds of things that quietly hold a site back. We fixed:

- **Duplicate page descriptions sitewide.** Google penalizes pages that all describe themselves the same way. We forced unique, keyword-rich descriptions on the homepage, the collections, and across the site — and wrote **23 unique descriptions for your product collections** (rings, necklaces, engagement rings, etc.).
- **Page titles.** Rewrote the homepage title, shortened over-long product titles, and fixed a formatting bug that was breaking titles.
- **The homepage had two competing main headings** — confusing to Google. Fixed to one clear headline.
- **Missing image descriptions (alt text).** For a visual, before/after brand this matters a lot — both for Google Images and accessibility. Added a sitewide fallback so no product image is left undescribed.
- **Structured data ("schema").** This is hidden code that tells Google and AI engines *what* your pages are — a product, an FAQ, a how-to, your business, you as a person. We added the full set (Website, Organization, Person, Product, FAQ, How-To, Breadcrumbs, item lists). This is what makes star ratings, FAQ drop-downs, and AI citations possible.

# Part 3 — What We Need From You

This is the important part. The foundation is built; now it needs **real photos, a few accounts set up, and your relationships.** Here's the priority list.

## 1. Photos — the single biggest thing we need ⭐

Your new pages have *spaces reserved* for images, but we can't fabricate your work. To finish the redesign hub and the blog, we need:

| What we need | Where it goes | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Before & after photos of real redesign projects** (the old piece next to the finished one) | The hub's before/after gallery + the *Before & After* blog post | This is the most persuasive content you have. Nothing sells a redesign like seeing one. |
| **A few process / in-progress shots** (sketches, gold being refined, a piece on the bench) | The service pages & blog posts | Builds trust and shows the craft. |
| **2–3 photos per blog post** (a finished piece, a styled shot, anything relevant) | The four new blog posts | Posts with images rank better and feel finished. |
| **Headshot / studio photo of you** | Hub + "about" context | Helps Google and AI recognize "Stacey Lorinczi" as a real person and authority. |

> Don't worry about perfection — natural light and a few angles on your phone is genuinely fine. Quantity and authenticity beat polish.

## 2. Testimonials & client stories

Even 3–5 short quotes from past redesign clients (especially heirloom/sentimental stories) would let us add a testimonials section — a proven trust-builder the niche leader uses heavily.

## 3. Two accounts to set up (free, ~30 minutes)

- **Google Search Console + Google Analytics (GA4)** — these let us see the *real* searches your new pages start earning and prove the traffic is growing. This is the foundation for everything we measure.
- **A Google Business Profile** (set up as a "service-area" business so your home address stays private) — this is how you capture *"jewelry redesign near me"* searches in the Bay Area. We'll guide you through both.

## 4. Google reviews from past clients

Reviews are the #1 lever for showing up in local "near me" results — and they build trust everywhere. A quick ask to happy past clients goes a long way.

## 5. The relationships only you can open (backlinks)

Backlinks — other reputable sites linking to yours — are currently the biggest thing holding rankings back. We've written the email templates and built the target list; what we need from you is the *go-ahead and the introductions*:

- "Best jewelry redesign" roundup articles (we find them, you're the name behind the pitch)
- Local partners: estate attorneys, wedding planners, antique dealers (heirloom referral sources)
- Press/"meet the maker" features in SF lifestyle and design press
