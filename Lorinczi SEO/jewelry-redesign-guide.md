# The Jewelry Redesign Guide

### How to Bring Your Old, Inherited & Unworn Jewelry Back to Life

*A practical guide from Stacey Lorinczi, Lorinczi Jewelry*

---

You have a piece of jewelry you never wear.

Maybe it's your grandmother's ring, a little too ornate for everyday life. Maybe it's an engagement ring from a setting that's dated, or a tangle of gold chains in the back of a drawer. You can't bring yourself to sell it — there's too much meaning in it — but you don't wear it either.

There's a third option, and it's the best one: **redesign it.**

Jewelry redesign takes the gems, the gold, and the story you already own and reimagines them into a piece you'll actually wear. This guide walks you through how it works, what's possible, what it costs, and how to start — so you can turn something forgotten into something treasured.

---

## 1. What "Jewelry Redesign" Actually Means

Redesign isn't repair, and it isn't buying new. It's reimagining what you already have:

- **Resetting** — moving your diamonds or gemstones from an old setting into a new one.
- **Repurposing gold** — melting down and refining unworn gold to use in a new piece (and crediting its value toward the work).
- **Full redesign** — reshaping a piece entirely, or combining several pieces into one.

The result is a one-of-a-kind piece built around *your* materials and *your* style — not a catalog item.

---

## 2. Five Things You Can Redesign

If it holds meaning, it can be reimagined:

1. **Engagement rings & wedding bands** — modernize a dated setting, mark an anniversary, or honor a marriage with a fresh design.
2. **Inherited & heirloom jewelry** — turn a grandmother's ring or a mother's necklace into something for every day.
3. **Old or broken gold** — refine unworn gold and apply its value toward your new piece.
4. **Loose or set diamonds & gemstones** — gather stones from several pieces into one design.
5. **Pieces that are "just not you"** — a gift or inheritance you love sentimentally but never wear.

---

## 3. The Redesign Process, Step by Step

A good redesign should feel collaborative and transparent — and these days, it can be done entirely remotely.

1. **Free consultation.** Talk through your pieces, your story, and what you'd love to wear.
2. **Share photos.** Send images of your jewelry and stones so the designer can assess what's possible.
3. **Custom design.** You receive a design built around your materials — and approve every detail before any work begins.
4. **Refining & crafting.** Your gold is refined, your stones are reset, and the new piece is handcrafted.
5. **Your new heirloom.** The finished piece arrives — made to be worn, loved, and passed on.

> **Tip:** Choose a designer who shows you the design *before* making it, gives you a clear quote up front, and ships insured and tracked. No surprises.

---

## 4. What Does Jewelry Redesign Cost?

Every redesign is custom, so cost depends on the design, the materials, and the labor. But here's the part people don't expect:

**Because you supply your own gems and gold, redesign often costs significantly less than buying a comparable new piece.** Unworn gold can be refined and its value credited toward the work, and you're not paying for a new center stone you already own.

The right approach: a transparent, itemized quote after your consultation — and nothing proceeds until you approve it.

---

## 5. Why Redesign Beats Buying New

- **Sentiment.** You keep the diamond from your grandmother's ring — the actual stone she wore — in a piece you'll wear every day.
- **Sustainability.** Reusing gold and gems avoids the environmental cost of newly mined materials.
- **Value.** Your existing materials reduce the cost of the new piece.
- **Uniqueness.** A redesign is one of a kind, designed around your life — not mass-produced.

---

## 6. How to Get Started

1. **Gather your pieces.** Pull together everything you never wear — even broken or incomplete items have value.
2. **Take a few photos.** Natural light, a few angles. Don't worry about perfection.
3. **Book a free consultation.** Share your photos and your vision, and a designer will tell you what's possible.
