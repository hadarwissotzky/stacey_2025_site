## Every estate has a jewelry drawer nobody knows what to do with.

The financial assets get divided. The house gets sold. And then there's the jewelry — appraised, documented, and handed to heirs who will never wear it, can't bring themselves to sell it, and quietly feel guilty about both.

I'm Stacey Lorinczi. I've been a designer and goldsmith in San Francisco for more than twenty years, and inherited jewelry is the heart of my practice: I redesign it — using the estate's own stones and metal — into pieces the heirs will actually wear. The sentiment stays; the form changes.

## What I do for families settling an estate

- **One estate, several heirs:** a grandmother's ring becomes a pendant for one daughter and the band becomes rings for two sons — the same materials, divided by design rather than by dispute.
- **A late spouse's rings:** resized to wear, worn as a pendant, or two bands made into one piece. I wrote a gentle guide for widowed clients: **lorinczi.com/pages/what-to-do-with-wedding-rings-after-your-spouse-dies**
- **Honest triage:** some pieces should simply be appraised and sold; I say so. Redesign is for the pieces that carry the person.
- **Careful process:** free consultation (online or in studio), transparent quote before any work, insured and tracked shipping, and every piece handled by exactly one person — me.

## Why this belongs in your client-resources list

The jewelry question has no deadline, which is why it never gets answered — heirs sit on pieces for years. A referral gives your client a concrete, pressure-free next step from a single accountable craftsperson, not a chain with a counter.
