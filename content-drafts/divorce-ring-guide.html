Should I keep, sell, or redesign my ring after a divorce?

There's no right answer — only the one that feels like relief when you imagine it. Some women tuck the ring away for their children. Some sell it and close the chapter. And many do what I help with: keep the diamond and the gold, but change the form — so the value and the beauty stay, without the ring.

What is my ring actually worth if I sell it?

Here's the part most people find out the hard way: rings almost always resell for a fraction of what was paid for them. Resale buyers pay for the stone and the metal weight — not the setting, the brand, or the story. If your ring's worth to you was mostly sentimental and financial, selling can still be the right call. But if the diamond itself is beautiful, redesigning often preserves far more of the ring's real value than selling it ever could. (If you do sell, get quotes from more than one buyer — offers vary widely.)

Is it bad luck to keep your wedding ring after a divorce?

No. Luck doesn't live in metal — meaning does, and you're the one who decides what this metal means now. That said, if the ring carries a weight you don't want in your jewelry box, that's not superstition talking; it's a real reason to transform it or let it go. Either is allowed.

Who keeps the engagement ring after a divorce?

In most divorces, each person keeps the rings they wore — engagement rings are generally treated as gifts, though the law varies by state, and anything contested belongs in your settlement conversation, not a jeweler's blog. If the ring is yours, everything on this page applies to it.

Can I redesign my engagement ring after a divorce?

Yes — and it may be my favorite kind of commission. The diamond was never the marriage; it's a stone the earth made long before either of you. Reset it, and it stops being that ring and becomes your jewelry. Women leave my studio wearing their same diamond as something they chose entirely for themselves — that's the point.
