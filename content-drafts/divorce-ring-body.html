Should I keep, sell, or redesign my ring after a divorce?

There's no right answer — only the one that feels like relief when you imagine it. Some women tuck the ring away for their children. Some sell it and close the chapter. And many do what I help with: keep the diamond and the gold, but change the form — so the value and the beauty stay, without the ring.

What is a divorce ring?

A divorce ring is simply a ring you wear for yourself after a marriage ends. Some people call it a disengagement ring, some call it a freedom ring or a self-love ring. Honestly, the name matters less than what it's made of and who chose it. More and more, women have one made from the diamonds and gold they already own — the engagement ring and wedding band become the raw material for something that belongs to nobody else.

What does a divorce ring look like? There are no fixed divorce ring styles, and honestly that's the best thing about them. I've made bold right-hand rings meant to be seen from across a room, and I've made plain bands with a date inside that nobody but the wearer will ever notice. Some women want a design that carries nothing of the original ring; others want the same stone in a setting that finally looks like them. Men ask me for these too — usually a simple band in a different metal from the one they wore.

And divorce jewelry doesn't have to be a ring at all. Plenty of women would rather wear the stone as a necklace and never put anything back on that finger — that counts, and in my studio it's the most common choice of all.

Plenty of women don't want any of it, either. Wearing nothing on that hand is also an answer, and it's a good one. Divorce rings are something some women want, not a step anyone is supposed to take.

What is my ring actually worth if I sell it?

Here's the part most people find out the hard way: rings almost always resell for a fraction of what was paid for them. Resale buyers pay for the stone and the metal weight — not the setting, the brand, or the story. If your ring's worth to you was mostly sentimental and financial, selling can still be the right call. But if the diamond itself is beautiful, redesigning often preserves far more of the ring's real value than selling it ever could. (If you do sell, get quotes from more than one buyer — offers vary widely.)

Is it bad luck to keep your wedding ring after a divorce?

No. Luck doesn't live in metal — meaning does, and you're the one who decides what this metal means now. That said, if the ring carries a weight you don't want in your jewelry box, that's not superstition talking; it's a real reason to transform it or let it go. Either is allowed.

Who keeps the engagement ring after a divorce?

In most divorces, each person keeps the rings they wore — engagement rings are generally treated as gifts, though the law varies by state, and anything contested belongs in your settlement conversation, not a jeweler's blog. If the ring is yours, everything on this page applies to it.

Can I redesign my engagement ring after a divorce?

Which finger do you wear a divorce ring on?

Most women wear it on the right hand — often the right ring finger, which feels deliberate without announcing anything to anyone. Some move it to the middle finger, some to the index. A few keep wearing it on the left and simply mean something different by it now. There's no convention to follow here, which doesn't happen very often — you're the first person who gets to decide what this ring means.

No. There is no deadline. Some women call me the month the divorce is final; some call three years later. The diamond isn't going anywhere — take the time you need, and when changing it starts to feel like something you want rather than something you should figure out, that's when you're ready.
