# ICP Growth Angles — Where to Find the Four People
_Idea bank, created 2026-07-25. Every idea pre-filtered through [ICP-COMMUNICATION-PLAYBOOK.md](ICP-COMMUNICATION-PLAYBOOK.md) ethics (no urgency, no proof-of-love framing, no grief-as-content without permission). ⭐ = recommended first experiments._

The common insight behind all of these: **these four people are not browsing jewelry sites.** They're mid-life-event — sitting with an attorney, a therapist, an estate, a support group, or an AI chatbot. The channel is wherever the life event already has their attention, and the person they trust in that moment (attorney, counselor, advisor) is the referrer who costs nothing.

---

## The referral-network play (the biggest untapped channel — all 4 ICPs)

Professionals who get asked "…and what should I do with the rings/jewelry?" *every week* and have no good answer:

| Professional | ICP they hold | The ask |
|---|---|---|
| ⭐ **Divorce attorneys, mediators, divorce coaches, CDFAs** (Certified Divorce Financial Analysts) | 2 | They literally divide the jewelry. A one-page "what your client can do with the ring" leave-behind + the divorce guide URL |
| ⭐ **Estate attorneys, probate attorneys, trust officers, appraisers** | 4 (+3) | Estates contain jewelry nobody knows what to do with; appraisers see it appraised then abandoned |
| **Grief counselors, therapists, hospice bereavement coordinators** | 3 | Softest touch required: a dignified card/printable, never "marketing." The lead magnet below is the vehicle |
| **Financial advisors specializing in widows** (a real, growing niche) | 3 | They manage the transition year; jewelry questions land on them |
| **Estate-sale companies & senior move managers** | 4 | They're paid to liquidate — but families always hold back "the good pieces" with no plan for them |

**Execution:** one warm outreach template per profession (Claude writes), Stacey/Hadar send 5/week to Bay Area professionals. This channel compounds: one attorney = a referral stream for years. It's also completely immune to Google/AI algorithm shifts.

## ICP-specific angles

### ICP 3 — Widows (trust is the channel)
1. ⭐ **"A Gentle Guide to Your Rings" — printable PDF lead magnet.** The widow page rewritten as a beautiful, unbranded-feeling keepsake PDF (playbook register). Triple duty: email capture on the page; the artifact hospices/grief counselors can hand out (solves "how do we partner without being gross"); shareable in the exact Facebook groups where the 860-comment threads live — *by members, not by us*.
2. **Grief-community presence, contribution-first:** Soaring Spirits / Camp Widow (annual gathering — sponsorship or attendance), Modern Widows Club chapters, Bay Area grief centers (e.g. Kara in Palo Alto — note: we have a Palo Alto page). Rule: contribute or sponsor quietly; never post marketing into a support group.
3. **Grief-podcast pitch** ("What's Your Grief" et al.): the story isn't jewelry — it's "what the research says about objects and grief, from a jeweler who's held those rings for 20 years." Stacey + the continuing-bonds framing is a genuinely good episode.
4. **The two-rings-into-one signature piece:** name it, photograph it, make it *the* thing Lorinczi is known for in this space (research: no measurable search volume, maximal emotional resonance — it spreads by story, not SEO).

### ICP 2 — Divorced women (reclamation is shareable)
5. ⭐ **A named divorce-redesign case study** ("before and after divorce ring" = 210/mo of *image* intent). One real client story, her words, shot for the before/after grid + Pinterest + a Reel. This ICP — unlike widows — often *wants* to celebrate publicly; reclamation content is inherently shareable.
6. **Divorce-adjacent media:** divorce-coach podcasts, "divorce glow-up" newsletters/communities (e.g. divorce support platforms run content constantly and need expert sources). Angle: "the ring is the last asset nobody plans for."
7. **Worthy/ring-buyer contrast content:** searchers comparing "sell vs keep" find buyers' content everywhere. Our honest "what selling actually gets you" section is the wedge — consider a standalone comparison later if the divorce page ranks.
8. **Second Saturday divorce workshops** (national nonprofit workshop series, Bay Area chapters) — guest-speaker slot or resource-list placement.

### ICP 4 — Heirloom inheritors (the estate moment)
9. ⭐ **"Inherited jewelry: a no-guilt guide" angle everywhere:** the emotional hook this ICP needs is *permission* ("a drawer keeps the object; a redesign keeps the relationship"). Reframe existing heirloom content/pins/posts around permission, not modernization.
10. **Estate-planning content partnership:** short guest piece for estate attorneys' client newsletters ("the jewelry clause: what families wish they'd decided early"). Positions Stacey at the *planning* moment, before the drawer.
11. **Multi-piece commissions frame:** "one estate → three pieces for three daughters" — case study + service framing. Highest average order value of any ICP; say it exists.

### ICP 1 — Upgrade couples (celebration is easy — just aim it)
12. **Anniversary triggers:** content + email segment around 10th/25th "the ring your life grew into" — original stones at the center (playbook: preempt upgrade guilt, never disparage the original).
13. **Vow-renewal adjacency:** officiants/planners as micro-referrers; a renewal is an upgrade moment by definition.

## What NOT to do (ethics line, from the research)
- No obituary-adjacent targeting, no funeral-home advertising, no "at-need" outreach of any kind.
- No grief-holiday campaigns ("this Mother's Day, honor her memory…" is the documented exploitation pattern).
- No urgency mechanics (countdowns, "book this month") on any ICP 2/3/4 surface, including ads.
- No client grief story used in content without explicit written permission; anonymity always offered.
